Aggregators, regret and rules

Aggregators

DecisionAnalysis.AbstractAggregator — Type
AbstractAggregator

Supertype of the aggregators: Expectation, WorstCase, BestCase, Hurwicz, Laplace, LowerExpectation, UpperExpectation, AlphaMaxmin, CVaR and Quantile. An aggregator turns each action's payoffs across states into one score; see aggregate.

They fall into three families. State-wise aggregators (WorstCase, BestCase, Hurwicz, Laplace) ignore probabilities and range over the states the belief allows: the states of a ScenarioSet, the one state of a PointBelief, and every state of the table for a FiniteBelief or a CredalSet, including states of probability zero, and every draw of a SampleBelief (so their value depends on which draws were sampled; Laplace is not defined for a sample). Single-prior aggregators (Expectation, CVaR, Quantile) need a FiniteBelief, a SampleBelief or a PointBelief. Prior-set aggregators (LowerExpectation, UpperExpectation, AlphaMaxmin) range over the points of a CredalSet and reduce to the expectation for a single prior. An aggregator applied to a form it is not defined for throws BeliefFormError.

DecisionAnalysis.Expectation — Type
Expectation()

Expected payoff under a single prior (a FiniteBelief, a SampleBelief or a PointBelief): Σ_θ p(θ) Q[a, θ].

DecisionAnalysis.WorstCase — Type
WorstCase()

The smallest payoff over the states the belief allows (Wald). Applied to a regret table it is the largest regret.

source
DecisionAnalysis.BestCase — Type
BestCase()

The largest payoff over the states the belief allows.

source
DecisionAnalysis.Hurwicz — Type
Hurwicz(α)

The Hurwicz criterion over states: α · best + (1 − α) · worst, where α in [0, 1] is the index of optimism. Hurwicz(0) is WorstCase and Hurwicz(1) is BestCase. Probabilities are ignored.

This is not AlphaMaxmin, which mixes the lower and upper expectations over a set of priors and, following its own literature, puts α on the lower one.

Which way α runs differs between texts. Here, as in Hurwicz's optimism index, it weights the best outcome. Several textbooks and software packages call α the coefficient of pessimism and put it on the worst outcome; a value from such a source is Hurwicz(1 − α) here. The independent Python reference of validation/ uses the convention of this package and says so in its fixture.

source
DecisionAnalysis.Laplace — Type
Laplace()

The unweighted mean payoff over the states the belief allows (the principle of insufficient reason). Probabilities are ignored.

source
DecisionAnalysis.LowerExpectation — Type
LowerExpectation()

The smallest expected payoff over a CredalSet, min_p Σ_θ p(θ) Q[a, θ]. The expectation is linear in the prior, so the minimum over the set is attained at one of its generating points. Maximising it is GammaMaximin.

source
DecisionAnalysis.UpperExpectation — Type
UpperExpectation()

The largest expected payoff over a CredalSet, attained at one of its generating points.

source
DecisionAnalysis.AlphaMaxmin — Type
AlphaMaxmin(α)

The α-maxmin criterion over priors: α · lower + (1 − α) · upper expectation over a CredalSet, where α in [0, 1] is the weight on the lower expectation (the index of ambiguity aversion). AlphaMaxmin(1) is LowerExpectation and AlphaMaxmin(0) is UpperExpectation.

This is not Hurwicz, which mixes the best and worst payoffs over states and puts α on the best one. The two share a name in parts of the literature.

source
DecisionAnalysis.CVaR — Type
CVaR(α)

Conditional value at risk of the payoff at level α in (0, 1]: the expected payoff in the worst α share of the probability mass, (1/α) ∫₀^α q(u) du with q the quantile function of the payoff under the prior. CVaR(1) is the Expectation and the limit α → 0 is the worst case over the support. Needs a single prior. Applied to a regret table it is the expected regret in the worst α share.

Conventions differ. This is the tail mean of a utility at the tail probability α, so a larger value is better. Software for financial risk works with losses and reports a positive number for a bad tail: the R package cvar gives ES(q, p_loss = α) = −CVaR(α) and VaR = −Quantile(α), and some texts index the level by the confidence 1 − α. It equals the optimum of Rockafellar and Uryasev's programme max_t t − E[(t − X)⁺] / α. Both are checked in test/test_validation.jl against cvar and against scipy's linprog.

source
DecisionAnalysis.Quantile — Type
Quantile(α)

The lower α-quantile of the payoff under the prior, for α in (0, 1]: the smallest payoff x with P(payoff ≤ x) ≥ α. Quantile(1) is the best payoff on the support. Needs a single prior. Applied to a regret table the bad tail is the upper one, so the result is the largest regret x with P(regret ≥ x) ≥ α.

source
DecisionAnalysis.aggregate — Function
aggregate(aggregator, problem::DecisionProblem) -> Vector

The score of every feasible action of problem under aggregator, in feasible_actions order. Payoffs are utilities, so a higher score is better.

julia> t = PayoffTable([:wait, :act], [:low, :high], [0 -100; -30 -30]);

julia> p = DecisionProblem(t, [3 // 5, 2 // 5]);

julia> aggregate(Expectation(), p)
2-element Vector{Rational{Int64}}:
 -40
 -30

julia> aggregate(WorstCase(), p)
2-element Vector{Int64}:
 -100
  -30

Regret

DecisionAnalysis.regret_table — Function
regret_table(payoff::PayoffTable) -> RegretTable
regret_table(problem::DecisionProblem) -> RegretTable

The Savage regret table R[a, θ] = max_a′ Q[a′, θ] − Q[a, θ]: what is lost in state θ by taking a instead of the best action for that state. Rows are actions and columns are states, as in the payoff table. Every entry is nonnegative and every column has a zero. The result is a RegretTable: a matrix that carries its labels.

For a problem, the maximum runs over the feasible actions only and the rows are the feasible_actions; for a ScenarioSet or a PointBelief the columns are the states the belief allows. Regret depends on the set of actions compared: adding or removing an action can change every entry of a column.

julia> R = regret_table(PayoffTable([:wait, :act], [:low, :high], [0 -1000; -200 -200]));

julia> R == [0 800; 200 0]
true

julia> R
RegretTable{Int64}: regret, 2 actions × 2 states
  action  low  high
  ------  ---  ----
  wait      0   800
  act     200     0
source
DecisionAnalysis.RegretTable — Type
RegretTable{T} <: AbstractMatrix{T}

A regret table with its labels, as returned by regret_table and relative_regret_table. It is a matrix (rows = actions, columns = states): it can be indexed, compared with a plain matrix and used in arithmetic like one. The labels are in the fields actions and states, the entries in R, and relative says whether it is a relative regret table. It prints as a grid of actions against states, and renders as a Markdown or HTML table in a notebook or a Quarto document.

source
DecisionAnalysis.relative_regret_table — Function
relative_regret_table(payoff::PayoffTable) -> RegretTable
relative_regret_table(problem::DecisionProblem) -> RegretTable

Regret as a share of the best achievable payoff in each state, R[a, θ] / |max_a′ Q[a′, θ]|, with the same layout and the same restrictions as regret_table. It is defined only when no state has a best payoff of zero; otherwise an ArgumentError is thrown. Unlike regret, it is not invariant under adding a constant to the payoffs.

source
DecisionAnalysis.expected_regret — Function
expected_regret(problem::DecisionProblem) -> Vector

The expected regret (expected opportunity loss) of every feasible action under the single prior of the problem, Σ_θ p(θ) R[a, θ]. Its minimum equals evpi and is attained exactly at the actions that maximise expected utility. Needs a single prior: a FiniteBelief, a SampleBelief or a PointBelief.

source
DecisionAnalysis.max_regret — Function
max_regret(problem::DecisionProblem) -> Vector

The largest regret of every feasible action over the states the belief allows (every state of the table unless the belief is a ScenarioSet or a PointBelief). Minimising it is MinimaxRegret.

Rules

DecisionAnalysis.decide — Function
decide(problem::DecisionProblem, rule; atol=0, rtol) -> DecisionResult
decide(problem, rule::EAdmissible; atol=0, rtol, optimizer=nothing, rounding_tol)
decide(problems::AbstractVector{<:DecisionProblem}, rule::ParetoFront; objectives, atol=0, rtol)
decide(tables, belief, rule::ParetoFront; feasible=nothing, baseline=nothing, atol=0, rtol)

Apply rule to problem and return a DecisionResult holding the score of every feasible action, the chosen actions and the rule.

For EAdmissible, optimizer and rounding_tol are passed to solve_program. For ParetoFront the objectives are several problems over the same actions, or tables (named payoff tables: a named tuple, a dictionary or name => table pairs) with one belief; objectives names the problems of the vector form, and the names are returned in details.objectives.

Ties. Every action whose score is within max(atol, rtol · max|score|) of the best is chosen. rtol defaults to zero for Rational and integer problems, where ties are exact, and to sqrt(eps) in floating point. The chosen actions are listed in the order of the payoff table and first_choice is the first of them; nothing is random.

A rule that is not defined for the form of the belief throws BeliefFormError: for example MaxExpectedUtility on a ScenarioSet. The set-valued rules EAdmissible, Maximal and IntervalDominance take a CredalSet; under a single prior all three return the Bayes actions.

julia> t = PayoffTable([:nothing, :levee, :relocate], [:low, :moderate, :high, :extreme],
                       [0 -10 -60 -150; -20 -20 -25 -90; -48 -48 -48 -48]);

julia> p = DecisionProblem(t, [1 // 2, 3 // 10, 3 // 20, 1 // 20]);

julia> decide(p, MaxExpectedUtility()).chosen
1-element Vector{Symbol}:
 :nothing

julia> decide(p, Maximin()).chosen
1-element Vector{Symbol}:
 :relocate

julia> r = decide(p, MinimaxRegret()); (r.scores, r.chosen)
([102, 42, 48], [:levee])
source
DecisionAnalysis.DecisionResult — Type
DecisionResult

What decide returns. Fields:

  • rule: the rule that was applied.
  • actions: the feasible actions, in table order.
  • scores: one score per action for a scalar rule; nothing for the set-valued rules over a credal set; the action-by-objective matrix for ParetoFront.
  • chosen: the labels of all chosen actions, in table order. Ties are all listed.
  • sense: :max when a higher score is better, :min when a lower one is.
  • details: a named tuple of rule-specific extras (see each set-valued rule, and MinimaxRegret for the note every regret-based rule attaches).

A result prints as a table of the actions with their scores and whether each was chosen, and renders as a Markdown or HTML table in a notebook or a Quarto document.

first_choice gives the deterministic single choice.

source
DecisionAnalysis.first_choice — Function
first_choice(result::DecisionResult) -> Union{Symbol,Nothing}

The deterministic single choice of a result: the chosen action that comes first in the order of the payoff table, or nothing when the chosen set is empty (possible only for Satisfice). Tied actions are never broken at random.

AbstractRule

Supertype of everything decide accepts: DecisionRule (which the named scalar rules construct) and the set-valued rules EAdmissible, Maximal, IntervalDominance and ParetoFront.

DecisionAnalysis.DecisionRule — Type
DecisionRule(summary; transform=IdentityTransform(), scenarios=nothing,
             selector=Optimize(), name=:DecisionRule)
DecisionRule(metric::RobustnessMetric; selector=Optimize(), name=:DecisionRule)

A scalar decision rule: a RobustnessMetric (transform, scenario subset, aggregator) that scores each action, and a selector that picks from the scores. name labels the rule in results and reports.

The named rules are instances: MaxExpectedUtility, Maximin, Maximax, MinimaxRegret, GammaMaximin and Satisfice. Others are one line, for example DecisionRule(Hurwicz(0.3)), DecisionRule(AlphaMaxmin(0.7)), DecisionRule(CVaR(0.1)) or DecisionRule(Expectation(); transform=RegretTransform()).

MaxExpectedUtility()

Maximin()

DecisionAnalysis.Maximax — Function
Maximax()

Maximise the best payoff over states: DecisionRule(BestCase()). Probabilities are ignored.

source
DecisionAnalysis.MinimaxRegret — Function
MinimaxRegret()

Minimise the largest regret over states (Savage): DecisionRule(WorstCase(); transform=RegretTransform()). The scores are the max_regret values, and lower is better.

Minimax regret depends on the menu of actions. It violates independence of irrelevant alternatives: adding or removing an action, even one that is never chosen, can change which of the others is chosen, because it changes the regret table (proved as minimaxRegret_violates_iia in the Lean development). A feasibility filter changes the menu in the same way. The result of decide carries this warning as details.note, with details.menu_dependent == true, and a DecisionReport repeats it.

source
DecisionAnalysis.GammaMaximin — Function
GammaMaximin()

Maximise the lower expectation over a CredalSet (Gilboa and Schmeidler's maxmin expected utility): DecisionRule(LowerExpectation()). Only the generating points of the set are needed.

DecisionAnalysis.Satisfice — Function
Satisfice(threshold; aggregator=Expectation(), transform=IdentityTransform())

Accept every action whose score reaches threshold: DecisionRule(aggregator; transform, selector=Threshold(threshold)). For utilities an action is accepted when its score is at least threshold; with a regret transform, when it is at most threshold. The chosen set lists all accepted actions in table order, the first choice is the first of them, and the set is empty (first choice nothing) when no action is good enough.

DecisionAnalysis.EAdmissible — Type
EAdmissible()

Set-valued rule over a CredalSet: an action is E-admissible when it maximises expected utility under some prior in the set (Levi).

The set is the convex hull of its generating points and an action can be optimal only at an interior prior, so checking the generators alone is wrong. For each action a, decide solves the linear programme

maximise t  subject to  Σ_k λ_k (E[a, k] − E[b, k]) ≥ t for every action b,
                        Σ_k λ_k = 1,  λ ≥ 0,

where E[a, k] is the expected utility of a at generator k. The optimum is at most zero and a is E-admissible exactly when it is zero. The result's details.witnesses holds, for each chosen action, a prior in the set under which it is optimal, and details.weights the convex weights on the generating points that give that prior (witness = Σ_k weights[k] · point_k). For a Rational problem both are exact, and export_eadmissible_witness writes them for an independent checker.

The programme is eadmissibility_model, and decide takes the keywords optimizer and rounding_tol of solve_program: by default the built-in exact simplex, otherwise any JuMP optimiser. Whatever solved it, on an exact problem with zero tolerances an action is reported E-admissible only with weights that have been checked exactly (nonnegative, summing to one, the action optimal under their mixture). details.exact maps every feasible action to whether its yes or no is an exact statement: always true by the default route and with an exact optimiser on an exact problem; with a floating-point optimiser a yes is exact (its witness was rounded and verified) and a no is numerical, to the tolerance of the optimiser, unless its optimum was certified by duality; on a floating-point problem every answer is numerical. details.route names the route (see ProgramSolution).

An action that is not Maximal is strictly beaten by another action at every generating point, hence under every prior of the set, so it is not E-admissible and no programme is solved for it (theorem isMixedDominated_of_not_isMaximal of the Lean development). details.dominated_by maps each such action to the action that beats it; its "no" is exact on an exact problem with zero tolerances. If a floating-point optimiser reports an action E-admissible and the rounded witness fails the exact check, ExactVerificationError is thrown.

source
DecisionAnalysis.Maximal — Type
Maximal()

Set-valued rule over a CredalSet: an action is maximal when no other action has a strictly higher expected utility under every prior in the set (Walley). Only the generating points are needed. Every E-admissible action is maximal.

source
DecisionAnalysis.IntervalDominance — Type
IntervalDominance()

Set-valued rule over a CredalSet: an action is kept when its upper expectation is at least the largest lower expectation of any action. Only the generating points are needed. Every maximal action is kept, so this is the largest of the three sets. The result's details hold the lower and upper expectations.

source
DecisionAnalysis.ParetoFront — Type
ParetoFront(aggregator=Expectation())

Set-valued rule over several objectives. decide(problems, ParetoFront()) takes a vector of DecisionProblems with the same feasible actions, one per objective, scores each action on each objective with aggregator, and keeps the actions that are not dominated: no other action is at least as good on every objective and strictly better on one. All objectives are utilities (higher is better). The result's scores is the matrix of scores, rows = actions and columns = objectives.

Optimize()

DecisionAnalysis.RobustnessMetric — Type
RobustnessMetric(summary; transform=IdentityTransform(), scenarios=nothing)

A robustness metric in three steps: restrict the problem to a subset of scenarios (state labels; nothing keeps all, see restrict_scenarios), apply transform to the restricted payoff table, and collapse each action's row with the aggregator summary. The subset is taken first, so regret is measured against the best action within the scenarios kept.

robustness evaluates a metric; DecisionRule adds a selector to one. Named instances: maximin_metric, maximax_metric, hurwicz_metric, laplace_metric and minimax_regret_metric.

source
DecisionAnalysis.robustness — Function
robustness(problem::DecisionProblem, metric::RobustnessMetric) -> Vector

The value of metric for every feasible action, in feasible_actions order. With the identity transform a higher value is better; with a regret transform a lower one is.

julia> p = DecisionProblem(PayoffTable([0 -1000; -200 -200]), ScenarioSet([:s1, :s2]));

julia> robustness(p, minimax_regret_metric())
