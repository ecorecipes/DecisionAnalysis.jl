Model-based methods, study designs and estimation

The methods that follow the R package voi and the reviews of Jackson et al. (2019, Journal of the American Statistical Association 114:1436; 2021, Epidemiologic Methods 10:20210012; 2022, Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application 9:95): a further regression, the estimators that run the model itself, standard study designs, value of information for a model that estimates a quantity and takes no decision, and the net benefit of sampling over sample sizes. The other methods of voi are on the page Sample-based analysis.

A further regression, and checks of a regression

DecisionAnalysis.MARSRegression — Type
MARSRegression(; degree=1, max_terms=21, knots=20, penalty=nothing, threshold=1e-3)

Regression by multivariate adaptive regression splines (Friedman 1991): the conditional means are sums of products of hinge functions max(x − t, 0) and max(t − x, 0), with the variables and knots t chosen from the draws. The basis is grown greedily, a pair of hinges at a time, each pair the one that most reduces the residual sum of squares, and then pruned term by term to the subset with the smallest generalised cross-validation score

GCV = (RSS / n) / (1 − C / n)²,   C = M + penalty · (M − 1) / 2,

with M the number of terms. One basis is chosen for all actions together (their payoffs standardised), and each action then has its own coefficients.

  • degree: the largest number of variables in a term. 1 is additive; 2 allows products of hinges in two variables.
  • max_terms: the size the basis may grow to before pruning, the intercept included.
  • knots: the candidate knots per variable, at weighted quantiles of the draws (all the distinct values when there are no more than that). earth tries every draw; a grid is the difference from it, and makes the estimate depend little on knots once it is a few tens.
  • penalty: the cost of a knot in the score; nothing is 2 for degree = 1 and 3 otherwise, the defaults of Friedman and of earth.
  • threshold: growth stops when a pair improves the fit by less than this share of the total sum of squares.

Unlike SplineRegression it chooses its own flexibility, and spends it where the payoff bends, so it suits conditional means that are flat over most of the range and change sharply somewhere, and several focal parameters of which few matter. A non-numeric column enters through an indicator for each of its levels but the first. The pruning is by a score, not a test: with few draws the fit can still follow noise, and the check of the estimate against the EVPI of the sample applies as for any regression (see VOIEstimate, compare_regressions).

DecisionAnalysis.regression_check — Function
regression_check(problem, focal, method; bins=10) -> NamedTuple
regression_check(problem, study::SummaryStatistics; bins=10) -> NamedTuple

Check the regression behind an evppi or an evsi by summary statistics (check_regression of the R package voi). What matters for a value of information is that the fitted values are conditional means: the residuals must average to zero wherever the fitted value is, and it does not matter that their spread varies or that they are far from normal.

For every action but the act-now one (the fit of an action enters the estimate only through its difference from another), the draws are cut into bins groups of equal weight by the fitted advantage over the act-now action, and the mean residual of that difference is reported with its standard error. The result is (; method, residual_variance, r_squared, bins, worst): bins holds one (; action, bin, fitted, residual_mean, standard_error, z) per action and group, and worst is the largest |z|. Groups whose residuals average several standard errors from zero show a fit that misses the shape of the conditional mean there; a regression that passes can still overfit, which compare_regressions tests on draws left out.

DecisionAnalysis.compare_regressions — Function
compare_regressions(problem, focal, methods; folds=5, seed=1) -> NamedTuple
compare_regressions(problem, study::SummaryStatistics, methods; folds=5, seed=1)

Compare regression methods for an evppi (or an evsi by summary statistics) by how well each predicts draws it was not fitted to. No method is reliable everywhere, and the estimate's own standard error does not show a bad fit (Jackson et al. 2022), so the comparison is by folds-fold cross-validation: the draws are split at random (from seed, the same split on every Julia version), each part in turn is given zero weight in the fit, and its payoffs' differences from the act-now action are predicted from the rest.

methods is a named collection or a list of regressions (SplineRegression, MARSRegression, GaussianProcessRegression, GAMRegression) or StrongOakley. The result is (; methods, best): one row per method, (; name, method, cv_error, cv_r_squared, value, standard_error, above_sample_evpi), where cv_error is the weighted mean squared prediction error, cv_r_squared its share of the variance explained (negative when the method predicts worse than a constant), and the last three are the estimate on all the draws; best is the name with the smallest cv_error. A method that fits noise has a good in-sample fit, a poor cv_error and a large value: prefer the method that predicts best, not the one that reports the most value.

Estimators that run the model

DecisionAnalysis.evppi_nested — Function
evppi_nested(payoffs, sample_focal, sample_rest; outer, inner, seed) -> VOIEstimate

The EVPPI of the focal parameters by two-level (nested) Monte Carlo, straight from its definition: for each of outer values of the focal parameters, the expected payoff of every action given them is the mean over inner values of the other parameters, and

EVPPI ≈ mean over outer of max_a (inner mean of a) − max_a (mean over all of a).
  • payoffs(θ): the payoffs of the actions at the parameters θ, a vector or a named tuple of numbers, in a fixed order. θ is the two samples merged into one named tuple.
  • sample_focal(rng): one value of the focal parameters, a named tuple.
  • sample_rest(rng, φ): one value of the other parameters given the focal ones φ, a named tuple. When the two groups are independent it ignores φ.

It needs outer × inner evaluations of the model and no regression, so it is the reference to check a regression estimate against on a model that is cheap to run, and too expensive otherwise (evppi with a method needs one sample). Every random number comes from Xoshiro(seed); seed is required.

The result is a VOIEstimate. standard_error is that of the mean over the outer values, with the inner means as they are. The estimate is biased upwards for any finite inner: the maximum over actions of noisy inner means overstates the maximum of the conditional expectations. bias.cells estimates that inflation from the covariance of the inner means (the actions share their inner sample, so their noise is correlated and inflates the maximum less than independent noise would), and is reported, not removed; increase inner until it is small beside the estimate.

DecisionAnalysis.MultilevelMC — Type
MultilevelMC(; tol, base_inner=2, pilot=200, min_level=2, max_level=10,
             max_outer=200_000)

Antithetic coupled multilevel Monte Carlo configuration (Giles and Goda 2019, Operations Research 67:1759) for nested evppi_nested and evsi.

At level ℓ = 0, each outer sample averages M₀ = base_inner inner draws; at level ℓ ≥ 1, each outer sample splits M_ℓ = M₀ · 2^ℓ inner draws into two independent halves A and B of size M_{ℓ-1} and accumulates the antithetic difference

Δ_ℓ = max_a Ȳ^{(ℓ)}_a − ½ (max_a Ȳ^{(A)}_a + max_a Ȳ^{(B)}_a) ≤ 0,

which cancels to zero whenever the same action wins in both halves. This gives variance decay O(2^{-3ℓ/2}) against cost O(2^ℓ), reducing the complexity of reaching RMS error tol from O(tol⁻³) for standard two-level Monte Carlo to O(tol⁻²).

Levels are added adaptively from min_level up to max_level until both the Monte Carlo standard error and the level-L bias indicator are below tol / √2.

DecisionAnalysis.MomentMatching — Type
MomentMatching(focal; payoffs, simulate_data, posterior, nested=30, regression=nothing)
MomentMatching(design::AbstractStudyDesign; payoffs, prior, nested=30,
               posterior_draws=1000, regression=nothing)

A study valued by the moment-matching method of Heath, Manolopoulou and Baio (2018, Medical Decision Making 38:163), for evsi. It needs the model and a Bayesian analysis of the study's data, and no summary statistic and no likelihood over all the draws, so it serves a study whose analysis is a model fit (a mark-recapture analysis, a hierarchical model).

The idea: the expected payoffs given the study's data are, to a good approximation, the expected payoffs given the focal parameters (the fitted conditional means of evppi) shrunk towards their mean, by as much as makes their variance equal to the variance that the study removes, Var(payoff) − E Var(payoff | data), estimated by running the analysis at nested data sets only. Both variances are taken over the same draws of the other parameters, so that their difference is not swamped by the variance that the study cannot touch.

  • focal: the parameters the study informs (columns of the draws).
  • payoffs(θ): the payoffs of the feasible actions of the problem, in its order, at a full set of parameters θ (a named tuple with every column of the draws). It must be the function the payoff table was made from; a table of noisy runs is not.
  • simulate_data(rng, θ): one data set of the study under θ.
  • posterior(rng, data): a sample from the posterior of the focal parameters given the data, a Tables.jl table with a column for each of them (any number of rows, a few hundred or more). The other parameters keep their prior: they are drawn from the draws at random, so they are taken to be independent of the focal ones.
  • nested: the number of data sets analysed. Thirty to fifty are usually enough; the cost is nested × (one analysis + the model twice at every posterior draw).
  • regression: the method for the conditional means given the focal parameters, as for evppi (default: chosen by the focal parameters).

With a built-in design (BinomialStudy, ...) the data, the analysis and the focal parameters come from the design and its conjugate prior.

The nested data sets are simulated at draws stratified by the fitted advantage of the most variable action (one draw at random in each of nested strata of equal weight), so that they cover the range that matters and its tails. For several actions the shrinkage is a matrix, which matches the covariance of the payoffs' differences from the act-now action and not only their variances; of the matrices that do, it is the one that moves the fitted values least (it is the identity when the study removes all that the focal parameters explain). The standard error combines the sampling of the draws with a jackknife over the nested data sets; the approximation itself (payoffs given the data being a shrunk copy of payoffs given the parameters) is a bias that no standard error covers. The method lives on variances: when the payoffs are strongly skewed, or the focal parameters explain only a small share of their variance, the variance removed is a small difference of two large and noisy ones and needs many posterior draws and data sets (details.variance_ratio, the share of the EVPPI variance removed, should lie between 0 and 1 and settle as they grow); the regression on a summary statistic, where one exists, is then the better estimator. If the estimated variance removed exceeds what learning the focal parameters exactly would remove, it is capped there (details.capped), since a study is worth no more than the EVPPI of what it informs.

Study designs

DecisionAnalysis.AbstractStudyDesign — Type
AbstractStudyDesign

Supertype of the built-in study designs: BinomialStudy, TwoArmBinomialStudy and NormalStudy. A design names the parameters of the draws that it observes and has methods of simulate_study, study_loglikelihood and study_posterior. It is turned into a study for evsi by SummaryStatistics(belief, design; seed), ImportanceSampling(belief, design; seed) or MomentMatching(design; payoffs, prior). A design of one's own subtypes it and gives the three functions methods, or is passed to those studies as plain functions.

source
DecisionAnalysis.BinomialStudy — Type
BinomialStudy(parameter; n)

A study that observes a yes-or-no outcome on n individuals: the count x1 of successes is binomial with probability the value of parameter (a column of the draws, a probability). In ecology: animals found infected among n examined, marked animals alive at the next visit among n marked, sites occupied among n surveyed (when detection is perfect). Its conjugate analysis has a Beta(a, b) prior, given as prior = (; a, b).

source
DecisionAnalysis.TwoArmBinomialStudy — Type
TwoArmBinomialStudy(first, second; n)

A two-arm study with a yes-or-no outcome, n individuals in each arm: the counts x1 and x2 are binomial with the probabilities named by first and second. An experiment with treated and control ponds, or a comparison of survival in two habitats. Its conjugate analysis has independent Beta priors, prior = (; a1, b1, a2, b2).

source
DecisionAnalysis.NormalStudy — Type
NormalStudy(parameter; n, sd=1)

A study of n measurements, normal about the value of parameter with a known standard deviation sd: the data reduce to their mean xbar, normal with standard deviation sd / √n. Its conjugate analysis has a normal prior, prior = (; mean, sd), worth (sd of the data / sd of the prior)² observations.

source
DecisionAnalysis.CountStudy — Type
CountStudy(parameter; n, dispersion=nothing)

A study of n independent count observations (transects, quadrats, traps, host examinations) with mean count given by parameter (a nonnegative column of the draws).

  • dispersion = nothing (default): n i.i.d. Poisson(λ) counts. The total count total ~ Poisson(n λ) is a sufficient statistic and has a conjugate Gamma(shape, rate) analysis (prior = (; shape, rate)).
  • dispersion = k (k > 0): n i.i.d. overdispersed Negative Binomial counts with mean λ and variance λ + λ² / k (size parameter k). The total count total ~ NegativeBinomial(mean = n λ, size = n k) is a sufficient statistic for SummaryStatistics and ImportanceSampling.
source
DecisionAnalysis.OccupancyStudy — Type
OccupancyStudy(occupancy, detection; sites, visits)

An imperfect-detection occupancy survey (MacKenzie et al. 2002, Ecology 83:2248): sites independent sites, each visited visits times. A site is occupied with probability ψ = θ[occupancy] and, when occupied, the species or pathogen is detected on each visit independently with probability p = θ[detection].

The pair (; sites_detected, total_detections) is a two-dimensional exact sufficient statistic for any sites and visits, so the design works directly with both SummaryStatistics and ImportanceSampling to trade off surveying more sites against more repeat visits per site.

source
DecisionAnalysis.CaptureRecaptureStudy — Type
CaptureRecaptureStudy(abundance, detection; occasions=2)

A closed-population capture-recapture survey (Otis et al. 1978 model M₀; Lincoln-Petersen when occasions = 2) over occasions ≥ 2 sampling occasions. abundance names the nonnegative population size N in the draws; detection either names the per-occasion capture probability p in the draws or fixes a known probability 0 ≤ p ≤ 1.

Returns the exact sufficient statistics (; unique_captured, total_captures) (with recaptures equal to total_captures - unique_captured for occasions = 2), supporting SummaryStatistics and ImportanceSampling.

source
DecisionAnalysis.MvNormalStudy — Type
MvNormalStudy(parameters; n, covariance)

A study of n independent multivariate normal measurements about the vector of focal parameters, with known positive-definite observation covariance matrix Σ. The sufficient statistic is the sample mean vector (; xbar_<p1>, xbar_<p2>, ...), normal with covariance Σ / n. Its conjugate analysis takes prior = (; mean, covariance).

DecisionAnalysis.study_posterior — Function
study_posterior(rng, design::AbstractStudyDesign, data, prior; draws=1000) -> NamedTuple

A sample of draws values from the posterior of the design's parameters given one data set, by its conjugate analysis: a named tuple of vectors, one per parameter. prior is (; a, b) for a BinomialStudy (a Beta prior), (; a1, b1, a2, b2) for a TwoArmBinomialStudy, and (; mean, sd) for a NormalStudy. The prior must be the distribution the draws of the parameter came from, or evsi by moment matching values a study against one belief and analyses it with another.

Estimation

DecisionAnalysis.variance_reduction_table — Function
variance_reduction_table(belief, inputs, outputs; method=nothing) -> NamedTuple

The EVPPI for estimation of every output against every input: which uncertain inputs drive the uncertainty of which estimates. inputs is a list of focal parameters (a name, or a tuple of names learned together, each as for expected_variance_reduction); outputs is a named collection of quantities (name => vector pairs, a named tuple of vectors, or names of columns of the draws).

The result is (; inputs, outputs, share, reduction, prior_variance, remaining_sd): share[i, j] is the share of the variance of output j that learning input i would remove (its main-effect or Sobol index, here read as a value of information), reduction[i, j] the reduction itself, prior_variance[j] the variance now, and remaining_sd[i, j] = √(prior_variance − reduction), the standard deviation of the estimate expected to be left. The shares of the inputs need not add up to one: inputs that interact share credit, and correlated inputs explain the same variance twice. This is the table behind a heat map of inputs by outputs (Jackson et al. 2019).

source
DecisionAnalysis.equivalent_binomial_sample_size — Function
equivalent_binomial_sample_size(mean, standard_error) -> Float64

The number of yes-or-no observations that an estimate of a proportion is worth: n = mean (1 − mean) / standard_error² − 1, the sample size of the binomial data that would give a Beta posterior with that mean and standard deviation from a flat Beta(0, 0) prior (Jackson et al. 2021). It turns a published estimate with its standard error into the prior effective sample size n0 of a GaussianApproximation, and into the a = n · mean, b = n · (1 − mean) of the Beta prior of a BinomialStudy.

julia> equivalent_binomial_sample_size(0.2, 0.1)
15.0

Population value and the net benefit of sampling

DecisionAnalysis.population_value — Function
population_value(value; population, horizon=nothing, discount=0)

A value of information per unit (per pond, per site, per person) scaled to everything the decision applies to. With horizon = nothing it is value · population: the decision is taken once for population units. With a horizon, population is the number of units that come under the decision per unit of time, the decision stands for horizon units of time, and later units are discounted continuously at the rate discount:

value · population · (1 − exp(−discount · horizon)) / discount,

or value · population · horizon with no discounting (the formula of pop_voi of the R package voi, which however returns value · population at a zero rate). value is a number, or a VOIEstimate, for which (; value, standard_error) is returned. The population and the horizon are assumptions about the future, and the result is proportional to both: show it for a range of them.

source
DecisionAnalysis.enbs_curve — Function
enbs_curve(estimates; n, setup_cost=0, per_sample_cost=0, population=1,
           horizon=nothing, discount=0) -> Vector{NamedTuple}

The expected net benefit of sampling of one study design at several sample sizes (enbs of the R package voi). estimates holds the EVSI at each size of n (numbers, or VOIEstimates as evsi returns for a GaussianApproximation with a vector of sizes), per unit. For each size,

ENBS = population value of the EVSI − (setup cost + per-sample cost · n),

with the population value of population_value. A cost is a number, or a 95% interval (lower, upper) (its midpoint is used), or (mean, lower, upper); an interval is read as that of a normal distribution, four standard deviations wide (as voi reads it).

One row per size: (; n, evsi, population_evsi, cost, enbs, standard_deviation, probability_positive, standard_error). standard_deviation is the uncertainty of the ENBS from the costs, and probability_positive the probability under it that the study is worth its cost (nothing when the costs are certain). standard_error is the Monte Carlo error of the population EVSI (nothing for plain numbers), kept apart.

As for enbs: only for additive payoffs, with the costs in the payoff's own unit (individuals removed by the sampling for a count; money only where the payoff is a published valuation), and never an invented cost. See optimal_sample_size.

source
DecisionAnalysis.optimal_sample_size — Function
optimal_sample_size(curve; near=0.05, smooth=false, df=nothing) -> NamedTuple

The sample size with the largest expected net benefit of sampling, from the rows of enbs_curve (enbs_opt of the R package voi). The result is (; n, enbs, lower, upper, smoothed): the best size, its ENBS, and the smallest and largest sizes whose ENBS is within the share near of the maximum (enbs ≥ max − near · |max|), the range of near-optimal designs. ENBS curves are flat near their top, so that range is usually wide and the smaller end of it costs little.

With smooth = false the search is over the sizes given. With smooth = true the ENBS is first smoothed and interpolated, by a least-squares cubic regression spline in √n with df coefficients (default: half the number of sizes, between 4 and 6; 4 is a cubic), and the search is over every whole size in the range: this removes the Monte Carlo jitter of the estimates, which otherwise picks a size by its noise. The smooth is only as good as the sizes given: they should bracket the optimum and be spread over the range, ten or more of them, not a few sizes decades apart. If the best size is the largest or the smallest given, the range was too narrow to find the optimum and the result says only that.
