Scenario designs

A scenario design is a set of scenarios built from a few axes, each either a decision (an intervention, under the control of whoever the design is for) or an uncertainty. This page follows Runge et al. (2024, "Scenario design for infectious disease projections: integrating concepts from decision analysis and experimental design", Epidemics), who classify designs by what their axes are and by their purpose, and apply the principles of experimental design to them: control, replication, pairing of replicates across scenarios, and fractional factorials.

purposeaxeswhat to do with the runs
decision makingdecisionscompare the actions: decide, gains over no intervention
value of informationa decision and an uncertaintydecision_robustness: does the better action depend on the uncertainty? With probabilities, evpi and evppi
sensitivity analysisuncertaintiesfactor_effects; with a belief, variance_reduction_table
situational awarenessuncertainties, at plausible boundsthe range of the projections
horizon scanninguncertainties, at extremesthe worst and best cases: Maximin, MinimaxRegret over the scenario set
forecastinguncertainties, with probabilitiesscenario_forecast

A design plans a table of runs: design_actions and design_draws go to outcomes, which pairs the replicates across scenarios by common random numbers, and DecisionProblem(design, table, utility) is the problem that the runs answer, over a ScenarioSet when the uncertainty axes have no probabilities. The model of a multi-model hub is one more uncertainty axis.

DecisionAnalysis.ScenarioAxis — Type
ScenarioAxis(name, levels; kind, weights=nothing)

One axis of a scenario design: a factor that differs between scenarios. kind is :decision for an intervention, under the control of the decision maker the design is made for, or :uncertainty for a factor that nobody controls. The same factor can be either, depending on the audience (vaccine uptake is a decision for an agency that can campaign for it and an uncertainty for a hospital), so the kind is stated, never guessed.

levels is a list of labels, or of label => value pairs (or a named tuple) when a level stands for a value that a model takes (:low => 2.2). Without values, the labels are the values. weights are probabilities of the levels of an uncertainty axis; they are needed only by a forecasting design and by expectations over the scenarios, and most designs have none. See decision_axis, uncertainty_axis and ScenarioDesign.

DecisionAnalysis.ScenarioDesign — Type
ScenarioDesign(axes...; purpose=nothing, audience="")

A scenario design: the scenarios are the combinations of the levels of its axes (a full factorial; see fractional_design for a part of it). What a design can answer follows from the kinds of its axes (design_class):

axesclasspurposes
decisions only:decision:decision_making: compare interventions
a decision and an uncertainty:value_of_information:value_of_information: is the better intervention the same whatever the uncertainty?
uncertainties only:uncertainty:sensitivity_analysis, :situational_awareness, :horizon_scanning, :forecasting

The four purposes of an uncertainty design have the same structure and differ in what the levels should be: plausible bounds for sensitivity analysis and situational awareness, extremes for horizon scanning, and for forecasting a set of levels that covers the uncertainty with probabilities (weights on every axis), so that the weighted scenarios make one forecast (scenario_forecast).

purpose states what the design is for and audience whom it is for; a purpose that the axes cannot serve throws an ArgumentError that says why. They are part of the design because the same scenarios read differently for different audiences, and are hard to recover afterwards.

The design is the plan of a table of runs: design_actions and design_draws are what outcomes takes, with replicates paired across scenarios by its common random numbers, and DecisionProblem(design, table, utility) is the problem the runs answer. See decision_robustness and factor_effects.

DecisionAnalysis.design_class — Function
design_class(design::ScenarioDesign) -> Symbol

The class of a design by the kinds of its axes: :decision (interventions only), :value_of_information (at least one intervention and one uncertainty) or :uncertainty (uncertainties only). See ScenarioDesign.

DecisionAnalysis.scenarios — Function
scenarios(design::ScenarioDesign) -> Vector{NamedTuple}

The scenarios of a design, one (; scenario, <axis> = level, ...) per scenario, in order (the first axis varies fastest). scenario is the label, the levels joined by _. A Tables.jl row table.

source
DecisionAnalysis.design_actions — Function
design_actions(design::ScenarioDesign) -> Vector{Pair{Symbol,NamedTuple}}

The actions of a design, for outcomes: one label => (; axis = value, ...) per combination of the levels of its decision axes. A model package's apply_action receives the named tuple. A design with no decision axis has the single action :projection => (;).

source
DecisionAnalysis.design_draws — Function
design_draws(design::ScenarioDesign) -> SampleBelief

The states of a design as draws, for outcomes: one draw per combination of the levels of its uncertainty axes, labelled by the levels, with the axes' values as its parameters (so with_parameters receives (; axis = value, ...)). The weights are the products of the axes' weights when every uncertainty axis has them, and equal otherwise, which is then a convenience for running the model and not a belief: use design_belief for the analysis. A design with no uncertainty axis has one draw, :all.

source
DecisionAnalysis.design_belief — Function
design_belief(design::ScenarioDesign)

What a design says about its states. A ScenarioSet when some uncertainty axis has no probabilities: the scenarios are held possible and nothing more, so state-wise rules and regret apply and an expectation over them does not. A FiniteBelief with the product of the axes' weights (the axes taken to be independent) when every uncertainty axis has them.

source
DecisionAnalysis.fractional_design — Function
fractional_design(design::ScenarioDesign, generators...) -> ScenarioDesign

A fractional factorial: the part of a design whose axes all have two levels in which each generated axis is tied to others. A generator :c => (:a, :b) keeps the scenarios where the level of c is the product of the levels of a and b, with the first level of an axis counted as −1 and the second as +1; :c => (:a,) ties c to a. One generator halves the design. The main effect of every axis can still be estimated (factor_effects), at the price that the generated axis is aliased with the interaction of the axes that generate it: the two cannot be told apart. This is how many uncertainties can be explored with few scenarios, each of which a model (or every model of a hub) must run.

source
DecisionAnalysis.factor_effects — Function
factor_effects(design::ScenarioDesign, values) -> NamedTuple

How an output differs along the axes of a design: the sensitivity analysis that a scenario design is. values has one number per scenario, the projected output (a mean over replicates, say), in the order of scenarios, or keyed by the scenario labels.

The result is (; grand_mean, total, main, interactions). main has one (; axis, kind, levels, means, effect, share) per axis: the mean of the output at each of its levels, effect the largest minus the smallest of those means (the second level minus the first for a two-level axis), and share its part of the sum of squares of the output about its mean. interactions has one (; axes, share) per pair of axes, for a full factorial: the part that their main effects do not account for and their joint means do. With two axes the shares of the two main effects and their interaction add up to one. In a fraction (fractional_design) main effects are aliased with interactions, and interactions is empty.

Every scenario counts equally: the shares describe the design, not a belief about which scenario is likely. For a share weighted by a belief, see variance_reduction_table.

source
DecisionAnalysis.scenario_forecast — Function
scenario_forecast(design::ScenarioDesign, projections;
                  probabilities=[0.025, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.975]) -> NamedTuple

One forecast from the scenarios of a forecasting design: the mixture of the scenarios' projections with the scenarios' probabilities (the products of the axes' weights). projections has, per scenario (in the order of scenarios or keyed by label), either a vector of replicate projections of one output, or a single number, its mean.

The result is (; mean, variance, between, within, weights, quantiles): the mean and variance of the mixture, the variance split into the part between scenarios (what the scenario axes are uncertain about) and the part within them (the rest, as far as the replicates express it; zero for single numbers), and the quantiles of the mixture at probabilities (nothing without replicates). A design whose axes have no probabilities is a set of conditional projections and has no forecast: that is the difference between the two, and this function throws.

DecisionAnalysis.decision_robustness — Function
decision_robustness(problem::DecisionProblem) -> NamedTuple

Whether the better action depends on the state: the reading of a value of information design (Runge et al. 2024), for a problem over a few states under any belief form. The result is (; best, robust, reversal, losses):

  • best: for every state, the feasible actions with the largest payoff there;
  • robust: the actions that are best in every state (often none);
  • reversal: true when no action is best in every state, so that the preferred action reverses somewhere along the uncertainty: knowing the state would change what is done, and resolving the uncertainty has value;
  • losses: for every action, its largest regret over the states, what is lost at worst by committing to it now.

reversal = false says that the choice is robust to the uncertainties that the design explored, which is a comfort only if those were the ones that matter; a design in which no uncertainty ever reverses the choice may have the wrong uncertainty axes. With probabilities on the states the size of what is at stake is evpi, which is positive exactly when the reversal has positive probability.
